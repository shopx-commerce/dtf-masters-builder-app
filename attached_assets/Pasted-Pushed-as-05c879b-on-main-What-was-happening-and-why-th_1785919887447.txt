What was happening (and why the earlier fix didn't help enough)
The last commit optimized the worker-side dedup, but Add-to-Cart had four hidden duplication penalties on the main thread that ran before the worker even started, and one of them silently defeated the worker dedup itself:

1. The main-thread HD decode step keyed by design.id instead of source Blob. For 80 total designs (4 unique × 20 copies), the loop was serially calling decodeBlobToImage 80 times to produce 80 different HTMLImageElements from just 4 unique source blobs. That's the biggest killer — likely 40–80 seconds all on its own before anything else happened. And because each duplicate got its own HTMLImageElement, the worker's WeakMap<HTMLImageElement, ArrayBuffer> couldn't cluster the 80 refs back into 4 sources, so we re-encoded all 80, transferred all 80, re-decoded all 80, and blew 80 stamp cache misses. My earlier optimization was effectively neutralized on this code path.

2. thresholdImageInfo (halftone pre-clean) was un-deduped — 20 halftoned duplicates ran 20 identical thresholding operations.

3. fileToDataUrl in buildDesignStatePayload re-read the same File 20 times (base64 of a multi-MB PNG is slow).

4. pdfDoc.embedPng (fluorescent path) re-parsed the same rasterized PNG per copy.

Fixes
All in useImageEditorModelCart.ts:

exportBlob → HTMLImageElement decodes are now keyed by Blob identity and run in parallel via Promise.all — 4 unique blobs = 4 parallel decodes.
New resolveExportImage(d) / resolvePdfImage(d) return the shared HTMLImageElement per copy, so the worker's identity-based dedup collapses N duplicates back to 1 source, 1 decode, 1 cached stamp — exactly the fast path it was built for.
thresholdImageInfo now goes through a Map<ImageInfo, Promise> cache so parallel Promise.all callers coalesce onto one promise per unique source.
fileToDataUrl calls in buildDesignStatePayload route through a readFileOnce(file) helper that memoizes by File identity.
pdfDoc.embedPng results are cached by (source, drawW, drawH, flip, alphaThresholded) for the fluorescent PDF path.

Rough estimate for a 3-minute cart export dominated by these serial penalties: expect under 30 seconds now, possibly under 15s depending on sheet size. The remaining time will be the actual worker raster + deflate pass plus the R2 upload, which are both proportional to output pixels (not to copy count).

If it's still slow after this, next place to look is the R2 upload itself — that could plausibly be another 30–60s for a 20–50 MB PNG depending on the customer's upload speed, and it's separate from the export.
